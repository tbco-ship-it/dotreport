Independent screening, not dispatch approval

DOT Report Card reorganizes public records. It does not certify safety, current operating authority, active insurance coverage, or suitability for a shipment. The index is not an FMCSA safety rating or CSA percentile and has not been validated as a crash-prediction model.

Source records and dates

We use the FMCSA Motor Carrier Census (az4n-8mr2), public SMS measures (4y6x-dmck), insurance filing history (c5y8-a4uz), and SMS crash file (4wxs-vbns) on data.transportation.gov. Search requests query these public files through our API; they are not real-time checks of every government system.

A weekly collection workflow and an optional manual refresh download all source stages into a new staging directory, validate them, and preserve the last published site if the refresh fails. Code-only deployments use the saved dataset. Source-file update times and the collection window are published after a successful fresh collection. Live API and saved benchmark dates may differ.

Benchmarks are comparisons, not legal limits

Each benchmark is total out-of-service inspections ÷ total inspections × 100 for the corresponding category across the SMS source file. Driver: {{ nat.driver_oos_rate }}%; vehicle: {{ nat.vehicle_oos_rate }}%; {{ '{:,}'.format(nat.carriers_in_sms) }} source-file carriers. These are weighted aggregate comparisons, not statutory standards or a validated peer-group adjustment.

Index v2.0.0

A grade requires at least five driver inspections and five vehicle inspections, valid OOS rates and benchmarks, available crash/fatality totals, a positive fleet count, and a recognized USDOT registration status. Otherwise we show NR — Not rated and withhold the numerical score. Missing data is not a passing result. Five is a product display rule, not a statistical confidence guarantee.

For eligible records, start at 100 and apply the following independent rules:

  • Driver OOS: −10 above the benchmark; −25 at or above twice the benchmark. Equality to the benchmark is not penalized.
  • Vehicle OOS: −10 above the benchmark; −25 at or above 1.5 times the benchmark.
  • Crash involvement and fatalities: −20 when reported fatalities (people) reach 0.5 per 100 power units; otherwise −15 above eight crash records per 100 units, or −5 above four. Compare crash records per 100 units rounded half-up to one decimal and fatalities per 100 units to two decimals. These are product-selected thresholds, not official risk cutoffs. They retain the earlier numerical mortality rule, now correctly labeled as people rather than fatal crashes.
  • USDOT registration: −40 for code I (inactive) in the census extract. Code A means active registration in that extract, not authorization to transport property for hire.
  • Operating authority, insurance and public SMS indicators: informational only; no deductions. A missing MC status is not a verified revocation. Missing insurance rows do not prove a lapse. An insurance-history row does not prove current coverage. Public indicator fields are not a complete BASIC alert assessment.

Clamp the result to 0–100. A = 90–100, B = 75–89, C = 60–74, D = 40–59, F = 0–39. Every grade still requires separate official checks; A does not mean all indicators are favorable.

Counts, coverage and known limitations

Fatalities and injuries count people. Fatal and injury crash counts are separate event counts, computed only when row-level source records are available. Older saved data and older API responses do not contain those event counts; we show “not available” rather than infer them from the number of people. Crash involvement does not establish fault or preventability; our index is not FMCSA's Crash Indicator BASIC.

Under the FAST Act, complete alerts and relative percentiles for property carriers are not publicly displayed. A source-file indicator, including an investigation-related field, must not be represented as a complete SMS alert check. Official FMCSA explanation.

USDOT registration and operating authority are distinct. A private carrier may have a USDOT number without needing an MC number. Official private-carrier explanation. We do not determine whether an exemption applies to a particular shipment.

Insurance data may include historical filings and incomplete coverage of carriers. Confirm current authority, coverage, limits and cancellation information in FMCSA Licensing & Insurance and with the appropriate insurer. Use SAFER and SMS for official records. FMCSA itself warns against inferring overall safety solely from SMS data.

Corrections and version history

Version 2.0.0, September 18, 2026, withholds grades for insufficient data, separates registration from authority, removes deductions based on unverified authority/public alert interpretation, labels casualty counts correctly, and stops claiming insurance or dispatch eligibility from the index. As a result, grades can differ from earlier versions even when source records are unchanged.

Report mismatched company identifiers or figures through Contact, including the USDOT number, source, date and field. Corrections to government records can be requested through DataQs.
